CRONOS: Benchmarking Counterfactual
Physical Consistency in Video Models

León Begiristain1 Olaf Dünkel2 Adam Kortylewski1
1University of Freiburg, Germany 2Max Planck Institute for Informatics, Saarland Informatics Campus, Germany

Abstract

Video prediction is increasingly viewed as a path toward generalizable world models, yet it remains unclear whether these systems learn underlying causal structure or merely exploit superficial visual correlations for future prediction. We introduce CRONOS, an intervention-based benchmark to evaluate counterfactual physical consistency: whether a model's predictions of physical events respond appropriately to controlled changes in the visual input, such as variations of camera viewpoint, scene, object category, and object appearance. Built in a photorealistic Unreal Engine environment, CRONOS enables controlled, high-fidelity generation of videos across diverse scenes and dynamics, including sampling multiple physically plausible outcomes of the same physical event. In contrast to previous benchmarks, CRONOS systematically intervenes on four factors — camera viewpoint, scene, object category, and object appearance — while keeping the underlying physical event type, such as a fall, collision, or occlusion, fixed. Our evaluation of recent open-source video generators reveals substantial failures in counterfactual physical consistency: prediction quality for the same physical event type is affected by appearance, environment, and particularly viewpoint changes. CRONOS provides a controlled and reproducible testbed for diagnosing how generated video quality changes across interventions, establishing a concrete target for developing models that perform consistently across changes of multiple conditions.

Core Concept

Counterfactual Physical Consistency

A model's ability to produce predictions of physical events that remain coherent across counterfactual variants of the visual input, such as changes in viewpoint, scene context, object appearance, or object category.

The CRONOS Benchmark

CRONOS frames video model evaluation as a controlled counterfactual experiment. Each physical event is rendered into multiple counterfactual observations by intervening on one scene factor at a time while holding all others fixed.

Physical Events

We span three fundamental rigid-body interactions, chosen to isolate distinct aspects of physical reasoning.

Fall

An object rolls across a surface and falls from an edge — testing prediction across changing contact conditions and free-fall motion.

Collision

One object impacts another — testing whether generated videos preserve plausible interaction dynamics, temporal/spatial coherence, and object permanence.

Occlusion

An object rolling across a smooth surface becomes fully occluded behind another object and later reappears — testing whether models capture long-range temporal coherence.

Multiple Plausible Futures

Physical parameters such as mass, elasticity, or friction cannot be inferred uniquely from one or a few conditioning frames, so a single "correct" future would unfairly penalize models. CRONOS therefore renders three rollouts of every sequence from the same conditioning signal, using a high, medium, and low value of a key physical parameter: the moving object's mass (collision), elasticity (fall), and friction (occlusion). During evaluation, each generated video is compared against all rollouts and scored against the best match. In total, the benchmark contains 2,025 videos (900 fall, 900 collision, 225 occlusion).

Systematic Visual Interventions

For each event, CRONOS applies one intervention at a time while holding the remaining variables fixed.

  • 📷
    Camera Viewpoint

    The rendering viewpoint is changed. Probes whether models disentangle scene geometry from observed motion.

  • 🎨
    Object Appearance

    Visual attributes (e.g., color) of the primary object are changed without altering physical parameters — isolating whether models disentangle appearance from dynamics.

  • 🏞️
    Scene

    The full scene is replaced — background, lighting, and event-relevant layout details change. Alters how the event unfolds across scenes, testing whether models adapt the event dynamics coherently to the new context.

  • 📦
    Object Category

    The object of interest is replaced with another, changing both visual properties and physical parameters (mass, friction). Probes generalization across instances.

Evaluation Metrics

CRONOS decomposes generation quality into complementary per-video metrics, validated against human ratings.

Background Stability — perturbations in environment over time, camera movement and appearance of new objects.
Appearance Stability — consistency of object appearance over time.
3D-Shape Stability — geometric consistency of objects throughout the video.
Motion Similarity — appearance-invariant motion agreement with the best-matching rendered rollout.
Physical Plausibility — VLM-as-judge (Qwen3-VL-32B) over physics-specific questions.
Success Rate — a video passes only if every metric clears its human-calibrated threshold and no object disappears.

To measure counterfactual consistency, we compute each model's sensitivity to interventions: how much these metrics change under an intervention, relative to resampling the seed.

Key Findings

We evaluate Cosmos2.5 (2B, 14B), Cosmos3-Super, CogVideoX1.5 (5B), MAGI-1 (4.5B), Wan2.2 (14B), HunyuanVideo-1.5, and MiniMax-H3 in the I2V setting, and Cosmos and MAGI-1 additionally in the V2V setting.

01

Models fail at basic rigid-body physics

All evaluated open-source video models struggle to reliably generate short clips of basic rigid-body physics. Even the strongest model (MiniMax-H3) achieves only a 36% success rate, with most models below 20%.

02

Visual stability ≠ physical correctness

Models that generate stable backgrounds and objects can still fail to produce the expected physical event. On falling objects, Wan2.2 has the best background stability and motion similarity, yet its physical plausibility falls below the calibrated threshold.

03

No model is counterfactually consistent

Generation quality changes substantially across interventions, especially for scene and viewpoint changes. The models with the highest success rates are generally the least sensitive (Spearman ρ = −0.87).

04

Video conditioning helps beyond motion

V2V improves not only motion fidelity (as expected) but also background and object stability and the consistency of generation quality across interventions.

05

Scaling alone is not enough

Scaling Cosmos2.5 from 2B to 14B parameters improves neither generation quality nor consistency under interventions (V2V success drops from 23% to 14%). Model size alone does not guarantee counterfactual physical consistency.

Limitations

  • Synthetic-to-real domain gap. CRONOS uses Unreal Engine renderings; this control is necessary for matched counterfactuals, but introduces a domain gap. A small-scale experiment with real recordings suggests that failure modes and general trends are shared across data domains.
  • Benchmark scope. CRONOS covers three basic rigid-body interactions. Since even the strongest evaluated model succeeds on only 36% of videos, harder phenomena would add difficulty without making failures easier to diagnose. Natural extensions include further rigid-body events (ramps, stacking, containment) and a non-rigid track.
  • Scope of evaluated models. We evaluate open-source models with fixed weights and reproducible settings, not closed commercial systems (Veo, Sora, Kling), to ensure scientific reproducibility and verifiability by the research community.
